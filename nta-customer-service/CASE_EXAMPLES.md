# Anonymised Case Examples

These examples are patterns only. They deliberately omit customer names, contact details, order numbers, addresses, and other identifying information.

## Case A — Livestock illness, partial loss

### Facts
A customer reported illness in a group of fish and supplied photos. Some fish survived. Evidence supported a genuine livestock problem, but not complete failure of the whole purchase.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `NTA` or `SHARED`, depending on evidence available in the specific case.
- Remedy: proportionate refund or credit for the affected livestock rather than automatically refunding unaffected items.
- Learning: document symptoms and quantity before calculating remedy.

## Case B — Delayed pickup of live food

### Facts
A customer delayed collecting a perishable live-food order after the agreed pickup timing.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `CUSTOMER` where the delay and communicated pickup conditions are clearly documented.
- Remedy: no automatic refund for deterioration caused by the delay.
- Devil's-advocate check: confirm NTA actually communicated the timing requirement before relying on it.

## Case C — Product quantity / weight discrepancy

### Facts
A customer supplied evidence that the delivered amount was materially less than represented.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `NTA` when the discrepancy is established.
- Remedy: a measured partial refund or store credit matching the demonstrated shortfall, unless ACL requires another remedy.
- Learning: improve weighing/packing checks and listing clarity.

## Case D — Wrong or missing item in an order

### Facts
The customer received something different from what the order record showed.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `NTA` when Shopify/order evidence confirms the mismatch.
- Remedy: replace or refund the item not supplied as ordered.
- Learning: verify line items during packing and record substitutions before fulfilment.

## Case E — Product listing overstated the item

### Facts
The listing image or description created a materially stronger expectation than the item supplied.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `NTA` where the representation is inaccurate.
- Remedy: replacement/refund proportionate to the failure, plus correction of the listing.
- Learning: fixing the customer case without fixing the product page leaves the root cause in place.

## Case F — In-store Betta selection followed by health concern

### Facts
A customer spent significant time comparing individual Bettas, asked questions before purchase, inspected the fish in person, and personally chose the animal. After taking it home, the customer later reported visible abdominal swelling. The fish remained upright and active in the supplied video. The customer also disclosed keeping the fish in its original transport water for several days before moving it.

### Decision
- Responsibility: `UNDETERMINED` at the initial review stage.
- Relevant NTA evidence: the customer had an unusually strong opportunity to inspect the exact individual animal before purchase.
- Relevant post-sale variable: prolonged time in transport water is a material husbandry variable and should be documented, but should not be asserted as the proven cause without evidence.
- Immediate remedy: husbandry correction, parameter check, monitoring, and request for an updated video if symptoms worsen.
- Do not automatically replace/refund while the fish remains active and causation is unresolved.
- Do not say that in-person selection waives the customer's rights.

### Devil's-advocate test
The strongest customer argument is that a latent health issue could have been present even though the fish looked acceptable at collection. That means NTA cannot rely solely on "you chose the fish" as a defence. The strongest NTA argument is that the customer personally inspected the animal and a significant post-sale husbandry variable exists, so immediate acceptance of liability is also unsupported.

### Learning
For individually photographed livestock, retain dated source images and clear care/acclimation instructions. Where possible, record that the customer inspected and selected the exact animal without turning that note into a liability waiver.

## Repository rule

Never convert a live customer case into a GitHub example until it has been anonymised. Internal business learning belongs here; customer-identifying data does not.
